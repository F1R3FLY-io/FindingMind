Chapter 14

Interventions at the Cut

14.1 What is being built, and why here

A program in rho with graded where-clauses over \(\Rnn\) already carries a causal model. The likelihood of a causal history is a product over firing sites of a locally normalized factor (Theorem 25.1 will take its derivative); each site’s factor is its clause; each site’s parents are the data its pattern and clause read; and the resolver that settles its race is the exogenous noise. This chapter makes the model explicit and adds the one thing Pearl’s calculus of causation has that the clause language lacked: an operator that intervenes. It follows the working note in which the construction was first set out [287].

The operator is a scoped decoration on parallel composition, \[\dok(\,y_1 \models \varphi_1 \ \&\ \cdots\ \&\ y_n \models \varphi_n\,)\ \ink\ P ,\] read as: whenever a race for the binding of \(y_i\) arises inside \(P\), resolve it among the candidates satisfying \(\varphi_i\), with the site’s own weights restricted to that set. It is placed at the cut because an aperture is a property of the cut and of neither agent flanking it (Remark 13.7), and because whether a particular cut will ever occur is undecidable even in the sequential fragment, so an intervention cannot be a surgery on a known node and must be a policy conditional on an event. The construct lowers into the where-clause by the normal-form theorem (Theorem 13.4), so it is a conservative extension, and it sits beside the cost membrane of Chapter 15 as a second index on a polymorphic cut.

With it, Pearl’s causal Bayesian networks over finite domains embed exactly: a variable is a join on its parents’ channels together with an ancilla race over its domain, the conditional probability table is the clause in disjunctive normal form, and the truncated factorization is the likelihood of a causal history. Intervening on the ancilla binding deletes the incoming edges on the nose, the three rules of the do-calculus are theorems about the embedded fragment rather than axioms, and counterfactuals are replay from a recorded pre-state under the intervention.

The chapter sits here, directly after Chapter 13, because it is the first thing one builds with a graded clause that one could not have built with a weight map, and because two parts of the book downstream need it: the mortal scientist of Chapter 25, whose hypotheses become rung-two objects once they can be placed under a \(\dok\) (§14.8), and the Prestige’s argument that a proof system or a learner that cannot inhabit a race cannot do what evolved intelligence does — for which this construct is the constructive content. Pearl himself has not appeared in this book before now, so the ladder is introduced before it is climbed.

14.2 Three rungs

Pearl’s ladder of causation [282] has three rungs, and a learner’s power is where it stands on it. Association: what one can learn from a record of what happened — \(\Pr(y \mid x)\), the distribution of completed runs. Intervention: what one can learn by acting — \(\Pr(y \mid \dok(x))\), the distribution when \(X\) is set to \(x\) rather than observed to be \(x\). Counterfactual: what would have happened in the run that actually occurred, had \(X\) been otherwise — \(Y_x(u)\), with \(u\) the particular draw of everything the model does not explain. Each rung is strictly stronger than the one below, in the sense that there are pairs of models agreeing at one rung and disagreeing at the next.

Definition 14.1 Causal Bayesian network¶

A causal Bayesian network \(M = (G,\{P_i\})\) over variables \(V_1,\dots,V_n\) with finite domains \(D_1,\dots,D_n\) consists of a DAG \(G\) on the \(V_i\) and, for each \(i\), a conditional probability table \(P_i(v \mid \mathit{pa})\) for \(v \in D_i\) and \(\mathit{pa} \in \prod_{j\in\mathrm{PA}_i} D_j\). It induces \(\Pr_M(v_1,\dots,v_n) = \prod_i P_i(v_i \mid \mathit{pa}_i)\). \(M\) is positive when every table entry is strictly positive. For \(X \subseteq V\) and an assignment \(x\), the submodel \(M_x\) replaces \(P_i\) by the point mass at \(x_i\) for each \(V_i \in X\) and deletes the incoming edges of \(X\), and \(\Pr_M(y \mid \dok(x)) := \Pr_{M_x}(y)\).

A structural causal model refines this by writing each \(V_i = f_i(\mathrm{PA}_i, U_i)\) with \(U_i\) independent exogenous variables; a CBN is a structural model with the \(U_i\) marginalized, and §14.5.3 recovers the \(U_i\) as the record of the resolver’s draws.

The reader who has followed the book this far will notice that the ladder is a statement about time. Association is what a trace shows; a trace is linear time. Intervention is what an experimenter who supplies contexts can learn; that is branching time, and context-labeled bisimulation is its equivalence. Counterfactual is replay with the record held. The alignment is made exact below, and it is the reason this chapter is in the Machinery part and not in a chapter on statistics.

14.3 Interventions have one carrier

Before adding syntax it is worth settling what an intervention can touch, since the answer constrains the syntax.

Definition 14.2 Intervention, semantically¶

An intervention at a receipt site \(r\) of \(P\) is a replacement of the map \(\Cand(P,r) \to \Semi\) denoted by \(\psi_r\) with another such map. It is hard when the replacement is the indicator of a single candidate, set-valued when it is the indicator of a subset, and soft otherwise.

Proposition 14.1 Deterministic communication is intervention-transparent¶

At a site with exactly one candidate, every intervention is either the identity on the normalized dynamics or disables the site. In particular no intervention at such a site changes which outcome is reached when the site fires.

Proof

The fibre is a singleton; a map on a singleton is determined by whether its value is zero. Normalization within the contention component sends any nonzero value to \(1\).

To intervene at a deterministic site one must change the fibre — inject a datum or delete one — and the moment a datum is injected the site has two candidates and is a race. The carrier of intervention is therefore the candidate set at a receipt, and a nontrivial intervention exists exactly where that set has, or is given, more than one element.

Proposition 14.2 No hard intervention by context over \(\Rnn\)¶

Let \(r\) be a race in \(P\). No rho context \(C[-]\) makes \(C[P]\) resolve \(r\) to a chosen candidate with probability \(1\) under the normalized \(\Rnn\) semantics, unless \(C\) consumes every competing candidate before \(r\) can fire — which is itself a race \(C\) wins with probability strictly less than \(1\).

Proof

This is Principle 13.1 read in the forcing direction. A context can add contention and consume contenders, and consuming a contender is a candidate at a site in contention with \(r\). Over \(\Rnn\) every candidate of positive weight has positive probability (Corollary 13.1 with positive constants), so \(r\) fires first with positive probability.

So over \(\Rnn\) a hard intervention requires access to the clause: one must be able to write at the site, not merely around it. This is the observation/intervention gap in its sharpest form — what a context can do is observe and perturb; what an owner of the code can do is set — and it is why the construct below is a decoration the programmer writes rather than a context one composes. One qualification: the clause controls which candidate fires and not when. An intervention on timing is outside the clause, and outside this chapter, unless time is reified as data — at which point it is a race again.

Principle 14.1 Interventions are metered because they are races¶

A race is a COMM and a COMM is charged. Reading the trace is free; intervening costs. This is not a policy choice: intervention has no carrier other than the event the ledger already prices.

14.4 The construct: a decorated cut

There is always a node in the syntax tree above a race, and it is a parallel composition: a race needs a receiver and at least two things it could meet. The cost membrane of Chapter 15 already made the cut polymorphic, indexing it by seals; an intervention is a second index on the same operator. And it cannot be anything more specific than a scoped decoration, for a halting reason: whether a particular cut will ever occur is undecidable, already in the sequential fragment, since “this receipt fires” is a nontrivial reachability property. So an intervention cannot be a surgery on a node known in advance. It is a policy conditional on an event — if a race for this binding arises, resolve it thus — vacuous if the race never arises. Pearl’s nearest object is a dynamic treatment regime rather than an atomic \(\dok\).

Definition 14.3 The \(\dok\) membrane¶

Extend the syntax of Chapter 13 with \[P ::= \cdots \;\mid\; \dok(\,C\,)\ \ink\ P, \qquad C ::= y \models \varphi \;\mid\; y \models \psi \;\mid\; C \,\&\, C ,\] where \(y\) is a pattern variable, \(\varphi\) a crisp formula and \(\psi\) a graded formula, each with \(y\) among its free names. Write \(\dok(y = Q)\) for \(\dok(y \models \crisp{y = @Q})\) and \(\dok(y \in S)\) for the disjunction over a finite set \(S\).

do( y = Q2 ) in { x!( Q1 ) | for( y <- x )P | x!( Q2 ) }

do( y1 |= phi1 & y2 |= phi2 ) in { P1 | P2 | P3 }

Definition 14.4 Membrane semantics¶

Let \(r\) be a receipt occurrence inside the scope of \(\dok(C)\ \ink\ P\) binding a variable \(y\) constrained in \(C\) by \(\varphi\) (resp. \(\psi\)). At \(r\) the clause is read as \(\psi_r \tensorv \crisp{\varphi}\) (resp. \(\psi_r \tensorv \psi\)), with the constraint evaluated under the candidate’s substitution, and normalization within the contention component is taken after the restriction. Reduction is \(\dok(C)\ \ink\ P \longrightarrow_w \dok(C)\ \ink\ P'\) whenever \(P \longrightarrow_w P'\) under clauses so read. A receipt in scope binding an unconstrained variable is unaffected; a receipt arriving in scope by reflection is constrained like any other.

Definition 14.5 Lowering¶

On a scope \(P\) in which every receipt binding a constrained variable is syntactically present and its channel is a literal or new-bound name, let \(\lfloor \dok(C)\ \ink\ P \rfloor\) be \(P\) with each such receipt’s clause replaced by \(\psi_r \tensorv \crisp{\varphi}\) (resp. \(\psi_r \tensorv \psi\)) and the \(\dok\) erased.

Proposition 14.3 Lowering is exact on flat scopes¶

If \(P\) contains no drop \(*z\) at a position that can come to contain a receipt, then \(\dok(C)\ \ink\ P\) and \(\lfloor \dok(C)\ \ink\ P \rfloor\) have the same weighted transition system, hence the same graded denotation and the same likelihood on every causal history.

Proof

Under the hypothesis every receipt that will ever be in scope is present in \(P\) up to substitution of names for pattern variables, which does not create or destroy binders. The membrane reads each such clause exactly as the lowering rewrites it, and the contention relation is unchanged because restriction removes candidates without adding any.

The gap between the two semantics is exactly the dynamic scope that reflection introduces: received code is the one route by which an unlisted binder — and, in the causal reading, an unlisted parent — enters. We take the membrane as the definition and the lowering as the optimization.

Observation 14.1 Restrict, not override¶

The membrane multiplies the clause by the constraint; it does not replace it. For \(\fork(y \leftarrow x \;\where\; \crisp{*y > 5})P\) under \(\dok(y = 3)\), the restricted clause is \(0\) on every candidate and the site waits; an override would force a \(3\). Pearl’s atomic \(\dok\) severs the mechanism and so is an override in spirit — but §14.5 shows that on the embedded models the two coincide, because the embedding puts the mechanism in the weights and the restriction is on an ancilla whose own gate is \(\top\).

Proposition 14.4 A compressed \(\dok\) is a weight map; composition¶

A constraint \(y \models \bigoplus_i v_i \tensorv \crisp{\chi_i}\) with the \(\chi_i\) pairwise exclusive denotes a weight map on the fibre at \(y\)’s site, and every weight map on that fibre is so denoted (Theorem 13.4 applied to the constraint). Moreover \(\dok(C_1)\ \ink\ \dok(C_2)\ \ink\ P\) and \(\dok(C_1 \,\&\, C_2)\ \ink\ P\) have the same weighted transition system, \(\&\) is commutative and associative, and \(\dok(y \models \top)\ \ink\ P\) has the transition system of \(P\).

So several interventions compress into one expression for the reason weight maps have a normal form: a formula has one value. The \(\dok\) is a scoped weight map placed at the cut, and the normal-form theorem is what makes it a conservative extension. It is also, in the vocabulary Chapter 25 will use, the operator that takes a specification — the \(\nu\)-property “every binding of \(y\) satisfies \(\varphi\)”, which one would check — and installs it in guard position at every site in scope, including sites not yet visible.

14.5 The embedding of Pearl’s calculus

14.5.1 The variable gadget

For a variable \(V_i\) with children \(V_{j_1},\dots,V_{j_k}\), allocate fresh channels \(v_i^{(j_1)},\dots,v_i^{(j_k)}\), one per child, and a readout channel \(v_i^{\circ}\); emitting \(V_i\)’s value \(d\) is \(v_i^{(j_1)}!(d) \mid \cdots \mid v_i^{(j_k)}!(d) \mid v_i^{\circ}!(d)\).

Definition 14.6 The gadget¶

For each \(V_i\) with parents \(V_{p_1},\dots,V_{p_m}\) and domain \(D_i = \{d_1,\dots,d_s\}\), allocate a fresh ancilla channel \(w_i\) and write 

new wi in {
    wi!(d1) | ... | wi!(ds)
  | for( p1 <- vp1_i & ... & pm <- vpm_i
       & u1 <- wi & ... & us <- wi
         where  [ *p1 = pa1 and ... and *pm = pam and *u1 = v ] * P_i(v | pa)
              + ...   // one summand per (pa, v) in the table
       )
    { emit_i( *u1 ) }
}
where vpj_i is the copy of parent \(j\) allocated to this gadget, the crisp bracket is the indicator embedding, and the sum has one summand per \((\mathit{pa}, v)\). The model term \(\sem{M}\) is the parallel composition of the gadgets under one new binding all the channels.

The join consumes all \(s\) ancilla messages, so there is no residual on \(w_i\) and no which-path record; the candidates are the \(s!\) bijections from \(u_1,\dots,u_s\) to \(D_i\), and the continuation mentions only \(u_1\), so the \(s!\) candidates fall into \(s\) outcome classes of \((s-1)!\) each. The clause is constant on each class. Under normalization within the contention component the \((s-1)!\) multiplicity cancels, and the probability that the gadget emits \(v\) given parent data \(\mathit{pa}\) is \(P_i(v \mid \mathit{pa})\).

Lemma 14.1 Staging¶

In \(\sem{M}\) every receipt is its own contention component, each gadget fires exactly once, and the causal order of every history is a linear extension of \(G\).

Proof

Every channel has exactly one receiver, fan-out giving each child its own copy, and every ancilla channel is private to its gadget, so no two receipts share a message or a receipt. A gadget is enabled exactly when all its parent copies have been emitted.

Theorem 14.1 Adequacy of the embedding¶

Causal histories of \(\sem{M}\) reaching a normal form are in bijection with assignments \((v_1,\dots,v_n) \in \prod_i D_i\), read off the readout channels, and the likelihood of the history for \((v_1,\dots,v_n)\) is \(\Pr_M(v_1,\dots,v_n)\).

Proof

By Lemma 14.1 a history is determined, up to the interleaving the causal quotient removes, by the outcome class chosen at each gadget, i.e. by the vector of values; conversely every vector is realized since every ancilla value is present. The factor contributed by gadget \(i\) is its restricted-and-normalized clause value, \(P_i(v_i \mid \mathit{pa}_i)\), and the likelihood multiplies the factors.

The truncated-factorization form is not imposed on the semantics; it is the semantics. The staging that makes each gadget its own contention component is the condition under which local probabilistic choice composes to a global measure at all [285].

Remark 14.1 The gadget is a stochastic matrix, and it has a quantum twin¶

Definition 14.6 is the one-qubit gadget of the complex instance (Chapter 38, [276]) — ancilla channel carrying the domain, a join that consumes everything, a continuation that re-emits without mentioning what was consumed, a clause in disjunctive normal form indexed by (input, output) — with three changes: the domain is \(D_i\) rather than \(\{0,1\}\), the inputs are several parents rather than one wire, and the matrix the clause realizes is stochastic rather than unitary. The resemblance is exact and is a statement about the semiring: the quantum gadget realizes every \(2\times 2\) matrix over \(\CC\) because the clause may mention both the input and the ancilla; this one realizes every stochastic matrix over \(\Rnn\) for the same reason, with stochasticity supplied by normalization within the component rather than by the clause. What differs is what one then does with the matrix: over \(\CC\) one arranges for columns to interfere and reads a detector that never fires; over \(\Rnn\) one reads the trace.

14.5.2 Surgery

Theorem 14.2 Graph surgery is restriction of the ancilla race¶

Let \(M\) be positive, \(V_i \in V\), \(v \in D_i\). Then \[\sem{\,\dok(u_1 = v)\ \ink\ \sem{M}\,} \;=\; \sem{M_{V_i = v}}\] as weighted transition systems up to structural congruence, where \(u_1\) is the first ancilla pattern of gadget \(i\). Hence \(\Pr_{\sem{M}}(y \mid \dok(V_i = v)) = \Pr_{M}(y \mid \dok(V_i = v))\) for every \(y\).

Proof

At gadget \(i\) the restricted clause is the original clause times \(\crisp{*u_1 = v}\), which is \(P_i(v \mid \mathit{pa})\) on the \((s-1)!\) candidates in the class \(u_1 \mapsto v\) and \(0\) elsewhere. The contention component is the gadget alone, so normalization divides by \((s-1)!\,P_i(v \mid \mathit{pa})\), nonzero by positivity, and the class fires with probability \(1\) for every value of the parents. The gadget’s normalized dynamics is therefore the point mass at \(v\) independent of \(\mathit{pa}\), which is exactly the mechanism of \(M_{V_i=v}\) at \(V_i\); the parents are still consumed, so the term is unchanged elsewhere; and every other gadget is unaffected by Proposition 14.3, the model term being flat. Theorem 14.1 applied to \(M_{V_i=v}\) gives the distribution.

The theorem is the reason to address the ancilla binding. Restricting a parent pattern instead would select on inputs — a conditioning — not sever a mechanism. The embedding fixes that the value of a variable is carried by its ancilla binding, and that is the whole of what makes the surgery exact: the gadget’s own gate is \(\top\), so restriction and override coincide, and the weights that encoded the mechanism are the thing normalization discards.

Remark 14.2 Positivity is starvation¶

If \(P_i(v \mid \mathit{pa}) = 0\) for some reachable \(\mathit{pa}\), the restricted gadget has clause \(0\) on every candidate when those parents arrive, and the receipt waits forever: the history deadlocks by starvation. This is exactly where Pearl’s \(\Pr(y \mid \dok(x))\) is undefined without positivity, and the embedding renders the undefinedness as a mortal computation’s death rather than as a division by zero. The reader of Chapter 25 will recognize the outcome \(\bot\) of Proposition 25.1: the environment did not answer.

Corollary 14.1 The do-calculus is derived¶

For positive \(M\) and disjoint \(X,Y,Z,W \subseteq V\), the three rules of the do-calculus [281] hold of \(\sem{M}\) under \(\dok\) as defined here:

  1. \(\Pr(y \mid \dok(x), z, w) = \Pr(y \mid \dok(x), w)\) if \((Y \dsep Z \mid X, W)_{G_{\overline{X}}}\);

  2. \(\Pr(y \mid \dok(x), \dok(z), w) = \Pr(y \mid \dok(x), z, w)\) if \((Y \dsep Z \mid X, W)_{G_{\overline{X}\underline{Z}}}\);

  3. \(\Pr(y \mid \dok(x), \dok(z), w) = \Pr(y \mid \dok(x), w)\) if \((Y \dsep Z \mid X, W)_{G_{\overline{X}\,\overline{Z(W)}}}\),

with conditioning read as post-selection on histories and \(\dok\) on several variables as the conjunction \(\&\) of their ancilla restrictions.

Proof

By Theorems 14.1 and 14.2 and Proposition 14.4, every interventional distribution of \(\sem{M}\) is the corresponding distribution of a submodel of \(M\), and post-selection on histories is conditioning in that submodel. The rules are Pearl’s theorem for the CBN \(M\) [281, 282].

The content of the corollary is not that the rules are true — Pearl proved that — but that the object they are stated for is literally present in the semantics, so that nothing is being modeled. The d-separation premises are statements about \(G\), and \(G\) is the causal order of the staged term; whether they are expressible as crisp-sort formulae over \(\sem{M}\), and so dischargeable by the same checker that discharges everything else, is the first of the open items in §14.9.

14.5.3 Counterfactuals

Pearl’s third rung asks what \(Y\) would have been had \(X\) been \(x\), in the world that actually occurred. In a structural model this is abduction (infer \(U\) from the evidence), action (mutilate), prediction (compute).

Definition 14.7 Exogenous record¶

A run of \(\sem{M}\) is a causal history together with, at each gadget, the candidate that fired. Its exogenous record \(u\) is the vector of fired ancilla classes. A run is replayed under \(\dok(C)\) from a pre-state \(S\) by executing \(\dok(C)\ \ink\ S\) with the resolver instructed, at each gadget whose contention component is unchanged from the recorded run, to fire the recorded class if it is still a candidate of nonzero restricted weight, and otherwise to sample.

Theorem 14.3 Counterfactuals are replay¶

Let \(M\) be positive, \(u\) the exogenous record of a run with outcome \((v_1,\dots,v_n)\), and \(S\) the initial state. Then replaying under \(\dok(u^{(i)}_1 = x)\) from \(S\) yields, at every gadget other than \(i\) and its descendants, the recorded value, and at \(i\) and its descendants the values \(Y_{V_i = x}(u)\) of the structural model whose exogenous variables are the ancilla draws. On the staged fragment the “otherwise sample” branch is never taken.

Proof

By Lemma 14.1 every gadget’s contention component is itself, and its candidate set depends only on which parent values arrived, every ancilla value being present. So the recorded class is always a candidate; it has nonzero restricted weight by positivity except at gadget \(i\), where the restriction forces \(x\). Non-descendants of \(V_i\) receive the same parent values as in the run and fire the recorded class; descendants receive the mutilated values and fire their recorded class, which is \(f_j(\mathit{pa}_j', u_j)\) for the structural model in which \(u_j\) selects the class — the standard reduction of a CBN to a structural model by inverse-CDF sampling, with the class index as \(U_j\).

The “otherwise sample” clause is where the general case lives: off the staged fragment an intervention can change what rests on a channel and thereby which candidates exist, and “the same draw” is no longer well defined. That the record of a run suffices for its counterfactuals is also the sense in which the history monad of Chapter 12 was always the third rung waiting to be read as one: \(\Hist\) records; replay under a \(\dok\) is the use of the record.

14.5.4 The dictionary

Table 14.1 collects the correspondence. Two entries deserve a word. Set-valued and graded constraints — \(\dok(y \in S)\) with \(|S| \geq 2\), or \(\dok(y \models \psi)\) with \(\psi\) graded — are not Pearl’s atomic \(\dok\) but the \(\sigma\)-interventions of Correa and Bareinboim [283], which replace a mechanism by an arbitrary new one and state their rules on the graph whose incoming edges at \(X\) are those of the new mechanism. Here the construct is natively a \(\sigma\)-intervention, and the new mechanism’s parent set is read off the free names of the constraint: a \(\dok\) whose constraint reads other variables is a policy, and its parents are written in the formula. The induced graph is syntactic, for the intervention as for the model. Conditioning is post-selection on histories, which costs \(1/\Pr(x)\) retries; the chapter on the complex instance met the same quantity as the price of a herald, and the ledger charges it the same way.

14.6 What it costs, and the fragment that tames it

The membrane semantics is simple to state and expensive to run, and the expense has three independent sources. The constraint language can contain modalities and fixed points, which cost what Chapter 13 said they cost: a clause that looks at successors is not local. The scope can be dynamic through reflection, in which case the \(\dok\) is a run-time membrane and not a rewrite. And restriction changes the normalization of contention components whose enumeration is permanent-shaped (Remark 13.12). A fourth question — whether a given \(\dok\) is vacuous, fatal, or neither — is reachability, hence undecidable in general.

The response is to grade the constraint language into fragments by evaluation cost and to impose type disciplines on the scope, and the disciplines are not new to this book. Staged: every receipt is its own contention component — the quantum-safe fragment of Chapter 38. Flat: no received code can come to contain a receipt — the hypothesis of Proposition 14.3. Finite-domain: channel types with finitely many values — what a language definition supplies for free. Confined: a \(\dok\) reaches only code its author has paid to deploy — the seal discipline of Chapter 15.

Proposition 14.5 The practical fragment¶

On staged, flat, finite-domain, confined terms with constraints that are indicators of finite sets: (i) \(\dok(C)\ \ink\ P\) lowers at elaboration and the membrane is erased; (ii) each constrained candidate costs one structural-congruence test per element of the set; (iii) vacuity and fatality are decidable; (iv) the induced graph is the static DAG with the constrained variables’ incoming edges deleted; and (v) the semantics is that of Definition 14.1 exactly. With constraints of bounded modal depth instead, (i)–(iii) hold and the semantics is a \(\sigma\)-intervention on a CBN.

Proof

(i) is Proposition 14.3, whose hypothesis is flatness. (ii) is the definition of the constraint fragment. (iii): under staging every constrained receipt fires exactly once and its candidate set is determined by its parents’ values, which range over a finite product; enumerate. (iv) and (v) are Theorem 14.2, whose hypotheses staging and finite domains supply.

That four disciplines introduced for four purposes — a quantum state, an exact lowering, a decidable check, an owner’s seal — draw the same line is the best evidence we have that the line is real. Outside it nothing fails; Pearl’s calculus stops and the calculus of this book continues. Off staging, the contention components are the branching cells of a confused event structure [286] and a \(\dok\) that touches one is a restriction of the cell’s distribution; off flatness, a \(\dok\) is a run-time membrane; off finite domains, positivity is a reachability question; off confinement, an intervention on another’s code is a soft intervention by Proposition 14.2 — an experiment rather than a surgery, which is what Chapter 25’s scientist does to its environment all day.

14.7 Execution, briefly

The construct was designed against the execution framework of f1r3node rather than for a simulator, and Part Part II examines that framework in its own right. Four facts are worth recording here so that the reader knows the construct is not only a semantics. Lowering is one more rewrite in the elaboration pass that already normalizes every deployment, and leaves no run-time trace. Off the flat fragment the \(\dok\) is a run-time membrane and the twin of the cost membrane: a scoped decoration on the cut, read off the term by the reducer, altering the COMM rule within; the two commute, since metering does not read the clause and the clause does not read the meter. An intervention is priced as one COMM plus the marginal cost of its conjunct, by Principle 14.1 — though fixing the charge against the ledger is owed (§14.9). And a counterfactual needs a pre-state and a record of fired candidates, which is exactly what a validator that executes a block and records the winners already has; the losing candidates are recomputable from the pre-state, so the same instrumentation that serves counterfactual replay serves the gradient of Theorem 25.1, one number per firing.

One observation from the execution model belongs here as well as in Chapter 24, where it is developed, because it is the dictionary’s last row. A shard in which one node executes and the others replay to confirm the state change is an experimenter/spectator split: the executing node resolves the races and so stands on rung two; the replayers observe and stand on rung one. \(\Pr(y \mid x)\) estimated from the finalized trace is the trace distribution and no more. That a distributed ledger is, by its design, a machine that separates the two rungs is not something its designers set out to build.

14.8 What this does for the scientist

Chapter 25 will build a learner whose hypotheses are graded clauses and whose experiments are guards. Everything it does is, in Pearl’s vocabulary, rung one and a half: it observes, and it perturbs its environment by composing contexts with it, and by Proposition 14.2 a context perturbs without setting. The \(\dok\) is what gives it rung two over the code it owns. A hypothesis is a clause; a clause under a \(\dok\) is a hypothesis together with the experiment that would test it; and — this is the point the reproduction section of that chapter will make exact — a germ carrying both is a hedge that is inherited, since a \(\dok\) is a term and crossover on quoted code applies to it unchanged. The construct of this chapter is what the scientist’s germs were missing in order to be rung-two objects rather than rung-one ones.

The Prestige takes this up as the constructive half of an argument it makes three times: that a system whose witnesses reduce confluently can state a non-confluent phenomenon but cannot inhabit it, so that a proof assistant of the usual kind, and a transformer trained on completed runs, both stand on rung one by construction. Rung two is a graded race under a \(\dok\); rung three is its replay; and the smallest behavior invisible to a trace — Milner’s two vending machines, which agree on every completed run and differ in a refusal — becomes a one-line program that, under \(\dok(y = \mathit{tea})\), either delivers tea or starves. That chapter is where the argument is made. This one is where the thing it leans on is built.

14.9 What is owed

  1. d-separation as a behavioral formula. Corollary 14.1 evaluates its premises on \(G\). Since \(G\) is the causal order of the staged term, the premises should be crisp-sort \(\nu\)-properties over \(\sem{M}\) — “no history has a causal path from a \(Z\)-firing to a \(Y\)-firing not passing through \(X\) or \(W\)” — dischargeable by the model checker. Stated, not proved.

  2. The rules off the staged fragment. Outside staging the contention components are branching cells and the event structure may be confused. The conjecture is that the \(\sigma\)-calculus rules hold on the confusion-free fragment with d-separation read off the event structure, and on the confused fragment per cell with the cell’s distribution in place of the mechanism.

  3. The price of a \(\dok\). Principle 14.1 says an intervention is a COMM. The marginal charge of the constraint conjunct, the budget charge of a modal constraint, and the charge for a counterfactual replay have not been fixed against the ledger of Chapter 15. Until they are, “an intervention costs one token” names an intention.

  4. Identifiability across namespaces. Pearl’s question — when is \(\Pr(y \mid \dok(x))\) computable from observational data in the presence of hidden variables — has a sharp rendering: the hidden variables are the code one cannot read, the boundary is the namespace (Chapter 19), and the observational data is the finalized trace. The identification algorithm [284] should transfer to the staged fragment directly; what it says about a shard is the question.

  5. Continuous domains and cycles. The gadget enumerates the domain on the ancilla channel; a continuous variable needs an ancilla carried as a density and the normalization become an integral, for which the Gillespie reading of Chapter 13 is the natural home. A recursive term has an acyclic event-level graph and a cyclic variable-level one; the cyclic structural models of the literature and the graded fixed point should meet there, with the budget as the unrolling depth.
